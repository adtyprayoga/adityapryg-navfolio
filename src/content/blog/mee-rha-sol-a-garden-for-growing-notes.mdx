I am building **Mee-Rha-Sol** as a garden for notes that grow over time. A note
can begin with a question, an observation, or a small explanation. As I learn
more, I can return to it, add a reference, correct an assumption, and connect it
to another subject.

Thinking of a note as a seed gives me somewhere to begin. I can capture an idea
while it is small, then give it more attention when I have something useful to
add. In Mee-Rha-Sol, I want to keep that process visible through explanations
that I continue to revise.

## The Name and the Sunflower

**Mirasol**, meaning sunflower, is the inspiration for the name. I bring three
personal associations into **Mee-Rha-Sol**: **Mee** means **"I am"**, **Rha**
represents the **Sun God**, and **Sol** means the **Sun**.

Together, those associations give the garden a personal identity. Mee places me
inside the learning process: I choose what to study, which ideas to question,
and when an explanation needs another look. The sun and sunflower imagery
express the direction I want to take toward clearer understanding.

I want that philosophy to show up in the notes themselves. A correction should
make an explanation more useful. A reference should help me check what I have
written. A connection should give me another way to approach a subject.

## Starting With a Seed

An early note might contain a term I want to understand or a question that came
up while reading. On a later visit, I might add an example. After trying the
idea in practice, I might find a limitation worth keeping beside the explanation.

I also want to connect notes across subjects. Something I learn while studying
one topic may help explain a problem elsewhere. Links let me keep those
relationships without forcing everything into one long article.

The garden gives me room for notes at different stages. Some need more
references. Others have enough detail to help someone work through a concept.
I want to keep improving them as my understanding develops.

## Obsidian Is My Workspace

I use **Obsidian** to capture ideas, organize sources, and work on explanations.
I can keep a rough thought close to the material that prompted it, then return
to both when I am ready to develop the note.

This is also where I connect related ideas and decide what needs revision.
Keeping the working material together helps me preserve context while changing
the explanation. I can follow a link back to a source or revisit a related note
before adding something new.

## Quartz Opens the Garden to Readers

I use **Quartz** to present selected notes on the web. Readers can browse by
topic, search for a concept, follow links and backlinks, or explore connections
through the graph.

Publishing remains selective. I prepare public versions of notes in Obsidian
and review what belongs in the garden. My working vault also holds rough and
private material, so sharing a note is a deliberate step.

Navfolio gives me space for longer articles such as this introduction.
Mee-Rha-Sol gives the smaller, connected notes a home where I can update them
as I learn.

## The First Planting

It gives the garden its first set of connections, while leaving room for other
subjects and the links I may find between them.

You are welcome to start with a note that interests you, follow a connection,
